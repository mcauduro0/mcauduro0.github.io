  1. DEC312026

    The 10-year: whether FRED's daily series closes at 5.50% or higher on any business day from October 16 through December 31, 2026, after a 5.31% high on October 5.

    Part 15: Oil Is the Headline. The Real Yield Is the Bill. Pendingp15-t01Pre-specified

    Question
    Does the 10-year Treasury constant-maturity yield (FRED series DGS10) close at 5.50% or higher on any day from October 16, 2026 through December 31, 2026 for which FRED reports a value?
    Source
    FRED, Market Yield on U.S. Treasury Securities at 10-Year Constant Maturity (DGS10), Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System
    Rule
    Read every daily observation of DGS10 dated October 16, 2026 through December 31, 2026, as FRED shows them on January 5, 2027; the days read are the days for which FRED reports a value. The answer is yes if any of them is 5.50 or higher; no if every one is below 5.50. Intraday highs, other maturities and yields reported in the press are read alongside and do not decide the test.
    If the source is late, revised or silent
    FRED posts each close about one business day later, so the observation for December 31, 2026 is due by January 4, 2027. A weekday FRED leaves blank because the U.S. bond market was closed is not missing and is not read: in this window, Veterans Day (November 11), Thanksgiving (November 26) and Christmas (December 25, 2026), by the holiday schedule of the Securities Industry and Financial Markets Association. Only a weekday on which the bond market was open and FRED still shows no value on January 8, 2027 is missing; if one is, and no observation already in is 5.50 or higher, the test is inconclusive, and the Result says what was read and what was missing; the next reading is January 15, 2027. A close FRED revises before the reading is read as revised; a later revision does not reopen the test.

    Part 15: Oil Is the Headline. The Real Yield Is the Bill. Pendingp15-t03Pre-specified

    Question
    Does the 5-year, 5-year forward inflation expectation rate (FRED series T5YIFR) average more than 2.60% over the days of March 2027 for which FRED reports a value?
    Source
    FRED, 5-Year, 5-Year Forward Inflation Expectation Rate (T5YIFR), Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
    Rule
    Average the daily observations of T5YIFR dated March 1 through March 31, 2027 for which FRED reports a value, as FRED shows them on April 1, 2027, rounded to two decimals. The answer is yes if the average is above 2.60; no if it is 2.60 or below. The 10-year breakeven (T10YIE), surveys of inflation expectations and readings outside March are read alongside and do not decide the test.
    If the source is late, revised or silent
    FRED posts the series about one business day later, so the observation for March 31, 2027 is due by April 1, 2027. A weekday FRED leaves blank because the U.S. bond market was closed is not missing and is left out of the average. Good Friday, March 26, 2027, is expected to be such a day: not a federal holiday, but a bond market close on the holiday schedule of the Securities Industry and Financial Markets Association, which FRED left blank in 2024 and 2025; the Result names every weekday left blank and why. Only a weekday of March on which the bond market was open and FRED still shows no value on April 8, 2027 is missing; if one is, the test is inconclusive, and the Result says what was read and what was missing; the next reading is April 15, 2027. Observations FRED revises before the reading are read as revised; a later revision does not reopen the test.
