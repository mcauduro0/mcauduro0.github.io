Does AI hardware hold its value?

Does the hardware hold its value?

From Part 10: The Bubble Is in the Denominator. 12 voices on 2 sides, attributed and dated; the DeepStack Call with its history; 5 dated tests that settle it; the results as they come in.

The mechanism

The S&P 500 trades at 19.0 times forward earnings, and the market looks cheaper because profits surged. Those profits depend on AI spending: capital spending becomes supplier revenue and reported profit, so the denominator of the multiple is partly the buildout itself, while about $250 billion of hyperscaler bonds issued through August compete for the savings that set the discount rate, with the 10-year Treasury at 5.31 percent on October 5. Whether the profits are real depends on whether the hardware holds its value: if used-chip prices fall once scarcity fades and server lives prove shorter than the schedules, depreciation grows faster than operating income and the earnings being capitalized belong to no one yet. The Call would change if chip values held, productivity turned higher and lab revenue compounded.

Alternative explanations

Four other readings.

What would have to be true for the Call to be wrong, as the other side argues it.

  1. The hardware earns because it is used: GPUs are already running hot while the data says adoption and utilization are still early, so the capacity is not idle collateral (the case David George makes).
  2. Old chips still rent at a price: contracted lease rates and second-hand prices have held for chips launched three to six years ago, and compute is about 60 percent of hyperscaler capex (the case JunkBondInvestor makes).
  3. The checks are arriving: OpenAI's recurring revenue is nearing $70 billion, with enterprise sales more than doubled since July, so the earnings being capitalized have an owner (the case Tae Kim makes).
  4. The rise is earnings, not multiples: tech's forward multiple is up about 14 percent since October 2022, against 250 percent in the run to March 2000, so this is not 1999 (the case Ren makes).

    Question
    Did the combined depreciation and amortization of Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 grow faster from the year-earlier quarter than their combined operating income?
    Source
    SEC EDGAR, the four companies' earnings releases (Form 8-K) and quarterly reports (Form 10-Q)
    Rule
    Sum depreciation and amortization, and sum operating income, for the four companies for the September quarter of 2026 and of 2025, from each release or 10-Q; a year-over-year growth rate for depreciation above the rate for operating income resolves confirmed, equal or below resolves refuted. A change to server useful lives disclosed in the same filings is the owner's reading into the Result, not the verdict.

    If any of the four has not reported by November 7, 2026, or restates the year-earlier quarter, the test is inconclusive, reviewed by the owner.
