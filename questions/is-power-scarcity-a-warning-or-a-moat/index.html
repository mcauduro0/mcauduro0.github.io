Is power scarcity a moat?

Is power scarcity a warning, or a moat?

From Part 06: Power Doesn’t Depreciate. Its Premium Moves.. 13 voices on 2 sides, attributed and dated; the DeepStack Call with its history; 5 dated tests that settle it; the results as they come in.

The mechanism

Electricity is the constraint, but the premium belongs to whoever can turn a contracted megawatt into revenue on time. Compute can be ordered faster than power can be delivered: NVL72 deployment at full use needs about 23 gigawatts by 2027, while grids, substations and transformers take years to expand, and Nebius quotes $40 to 50 million for six months of capacity against $20 to 25 million for one to three years. A megawatt generates rent only when it arrives on schedule; a GPU parked behind a late interconnection generates cost. That is why the Call reads scarcity as a moat for the permit and the interconnect rather than for the hardware, and why it would change if the premium narrowed while power stayed scarce or a hyperscaler canceled because compute was sufficient.

Alternative explanations

Three other readings.

What would have to be true for the Call to be wrong, as the other side argues it.

  1. Permission is the asset politics can revoke: 70 to 75 percent of Americans oppose large data centers near their homes and more than half back a national moratorium (the case Michael Spencer makes).
  2. Scarcity is the warning fiber gave in 1999: capacity built on the expectation that demand keeps outrunning supply goes dark once a technical step multiplies what each unit can carry (the case Gaetano makes).
  3. The moat is financed, not owned: the builder's assets go obsolete faster than the buildings it leases, its largest customers are competing clouds and its only chip supplier is an investor (the case Kakashii makes).
