  1. OCT282026

    Alphabet's September quarter: whether capital spending takes 80 cents or more of each dollar of operating cash, after 49.5 cents a year earlier and 95.0 cents in the first half of 2026.

    Part 14: The Cash Arrives Unevenly Pendingp14-t01Pre-specified

    Question
    Did Alphabet's purchases of property and equipment for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 equal 80% or more of its net cash provided by operating activities for the same quarter?
    Source
    SEC EDGAR, Alphabet filings (the earnings release on Form 8-K and the Form 10-Q for the quarter), CIK 0001652044
    Rule
    Take the three-month figures from the statement of cash flows in Alphabet's earnings release; if the release gives only year-to-date figures, use the nine-month figure less the six-month figure from the Form 10-Q. Divide purchases of property and equipment by net cash provided by operating activities. The answer is yes if the ratio is 80.0% or more; no if it is below 80.0%. Alphabet's own free cash flow line, finance leases and its spending guidance for the year are read alongside and do not decide the test.
    If the source is late, revised or silent
    Alphabet has scheduled the release for October 28, 2026. If neither the release nor a Form 10-Q states the quarter's cash flow figures on EDGAR by November 9, 2026, the test is inconclusive, and the Result says what was read and what was missing; the next reading is November 16, 2026. Figures are read as first filed; a later amendment does not reopen the test.

    Part 14: The Cash Arrives Unevenly Pendingp14-t02Pre-specified

    Question
    Did the combined purchases of property and equipment of Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 exceed their combined net cash provided by operating activities for the same quarter?
    Source
    SEC EDGAR, the Forms 10-Q of Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta for the quarter (statements of cash flows; XBRL company facts)
    Rule
    Read each company's statement of cash flows for the quarter; where a Form 10-Q gives only year-to-date figures, the quarter is the later cumulative figure less the earlier one from the same fiscal year (for Microsoft, the first quarter of its fiscal 2027). For Amazon, purchases are the gross line, before proceeds from sales and incentives. Sum the four purchases and the four operating figures. The answer is yes if combined purchases exceed combined net cash provided by operating activities; no if they are equal or lower. Each company's own ratio and free cash flow measure are read alongside and do not decide the test.

    If any of the four Forms 10-Q is not on EDGAR by November 9, 2026, the filing deadline for large companies, the test is inconclusive on November 10, and the Result says what was read and what was missing; the next reading is November 16, 2026. Figures are read as first filed; a restated earlier quarter in the same filings is named in the Result and does not change the reading.

    Source
    Investor-relations pages of Alphabet (abc.xyz/investor), Amazon (ir.aboutamazon.com) and Meta (investor.atmeta.com): fourth-quarter 2026 earnings releases and call transcripts
    Rule
    Each company's 2026 capital expenditures and its 2027 guidance are read as that company defines capital expenditures in the release or on the call that gives the guidance (for Meta, including principal payments on finance leases). The answer is yes if the three 2027 figures sum to 1.25 times the three 2026 figures or more; no if the sum is below that. Microsoft gives no calendar-year figure and is not part of the test.
