Is AI debt 1999, 2008 or neither?

The debt behind the machines: 1999 capex, 2008 credit, or neither?

From Part 03: The Machines Will Work. The Debt Might Not.. 7 voices on 2 sides, attributed and dated; the DeepStack Call with its history; 5 dated tests that settle it; the results as they come in.

The mechanism

The machines will earn; the question is who carries the paper behind them. Nebius's own terms put compute in backwardation: buyers pay roughly twice as much for six months or less as for one to three years, $40 to 50 million against $20 to 25 million, a signal about duration that a lender cannot ignore. The 95 percent H100 re-let and a modeled 21 percent return keep the bull case credible, yet neither settles who bears depreciation when financing costs rise, and only 6 percent of the AI paper issued is investment grade. So the credit stack, not the demand, is where the buildout breaks first: if the short-term premium persists and the queued $50 to 60 billion of issuance needs wider concessions to clear, lenders are repricing the risk before the machines stop earning.

Alternative explanations

Three other readings.

What would have to be true for the Call to be wrong, as the other side argues it.

  1. The hardware is holding its price: rents on six-year-old A100s are up more than 20 percent this year, a direct read on willingness to pay that the backwardation signal does not capture (the case Oguz Erkan makes).
  2. Capacity is sold before it is built, so the borrowing is rational: $496 billion of backlog at AWS, $678 billion at Microsoft and $514 billion at Google Cloud (the case Market Sentiment makes).
  3. Open and Chinese models deliver comparable results at a fraction of the cost, so data-center pricing faces the race to the bottom that fiber ran after 1999 (the case Vitaliy Katsenelson makes).

    Question
    Did Nebius's September-quarter results, in the release, the shareholder letter or on the call, still price compute capacity of six months or less at a premium to its one-to-three-year contracts?
    Source
    Nebius Group, quarterly results furnished to SEC EDGAR (Form 6-K)
    Rule
    Read the price per megawatt (or per GPU-hour) Nebius gives for terms of six months or less and for one-to-three-year terms; a short-term price at or above 1.5 times the long-term price resolves confirmed (the premium persists), a ratio below 1.5 resolves refuted (the premium collapsed toward long-term rates).
    If the source is late, revised or silent
    If the results carry no term pricing, or Nebius reports after November 30, 2026, the test is inconclusive, reviewed by the owner.

Confirmed

Oct 10, 2026 · Part 03: The Machines Will Work. The Debt Might Not. · Test p03-t04

Confirmed: the investment-grade spread ended the window five basis points wider than August's average, as the Call expected.

The test. The next $50B-$60B issuance will show whether new-issue concessions keep widening or start to normalize.

The ICE BofA US Corporate Index option-adjusted spread read 0.85% on October 2, 2026, the end of the four weeks after Labor Day, against an average of 0.80% over August's 21 daily readings (FRED series BAMLC0A0CM). The difference is +0.05%, five basis points, outside the 3 basis point band the rule names, so the answer to the question is no: the market did not price the window tighter than August. That is the outcome the Call expected of the credit stack.

What the series does not show, the press did. On September 22 Reuters reported spreads on AI-related issuers near 115 basis points against 78 for the broader investment-grade market, Alphabet paying a large concession on its August sale, and Aon's $13.5 billion financing drawing $65 billion of orders with its 30-year tranche tightening 35 basis points. Concessions normalized away from the AI complex and stayed wide inside it. That split is evidence here, not the rule.

The Call of Part 03, bearish on the credit of the buildout, expected the spread to widen, so a refuted test on this question is the market agreeing with the Call.
