THE QUESTION
Did Nebius's September-quarter results, in the release, the shareholder letter or on the call, still price compute capacity of six months or less at a premium to its one-to-three-year contracts?

HOW IT IS SETTLED
Rule: Read the price per megawatt (or per GPU-hour) Nebius gives for terms of six months or less and for one-to-three-year terms. The answer is yes if the short-term price is at or above 1.5 times the long-term price (the premium persists), and no if the ratio is below 1.5 (the premium collapsed toward long-term rates).
Source: Nebius Group, quarterly results furnished to SEC EDGAR (Form 6-K) (https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=NBIS&type=6-K)
If the reading is unclear: If the results carry no term pricing, or Nebius reports after November 30, 2026, the test is inconclusive, and the Result says what was read and what was missing.
Test date: 2026-11-10 (Next Nebius disclosure); the test closes at 00:00 UTC that day

THE PAGE: Part 3, "The Machines Will Work. The Debt Might Not."
AI demand may be real. The more dangerous question is who is financing the machines, and how quickly lenders can change their minds.
What the page says this test would show: Short-term premiums collapsing toward long-term rates would weaken the backwardation warning; persistence would make it harder to dismiss.
