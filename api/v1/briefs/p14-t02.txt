THE QUESTION
Did the combined purchases of property and equipment of Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 exceed their combined net cash provided by operating activities for the same quarter?

HOW IT IS SETTLED
Rule: Read each company's statement of cash flows for the quarter; where a Form 10-Q gives only year-to-date figures, the quarter is the later cumulative figure less the earlier one from the same fiscal year (for Microsoft, the first quarter of its fiscal 2027). For Amazon, purchases are the gross line, before proceeds from sales and incentives. Sum the four purchases and the four operating figures. The answer is yes if combined purchases exceed combined net cash provided by operating activities; no if they are equal or lower. Each company's own ratio and free cash flow measure are read alongside and do not decide the test.
Source: SEC EDGAR, the Forms 10-Q of Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta for the quarter (statements of cash flows; XBRL company facts) (https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/)
If the reading is unclear: If any of the four Forms 10-Q is not on EDGAR by November 9, 2026, the filing deadline for large companies, the test is inconclusive on November 10, and the Result says what was read and what was missing; the next reading is November 16, 2026. Figures are read as first filed; a restated earlier quarter in the same filings is named in the Result and does not change the reading.
Test date: 2026-11-10 (November 10); the test closes at 00:00 UTC that day

THE PAGE: Part 14, "The Cash Arrives Unevenly"
Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta spent 96 cents on property and equipment for each dollar of operating cash in the second quarter of 2026, up from 65 cents across 2025, by DeepStack's reading of SEC filings. Microsoft still pays from its own cash. Amazon spent $1.38 per dollar in the first half.
What the page says this test would show: The four's September-quarter reports on EDGAR: whether combined purchases of property and equipment topped combined operating cash, after 96.1% in the June quarter of 2026.

THE CALL (DeepStack's house view, in words)
Stance: Only Microsoft pays with room to spare
Line: Microsoft pays for its build from its own cash. Alphabet and Meta spend most of theirs; Amazon spends more than its operations bring in.
What would change the Call:
- Alphabet spends under 80 cents of each operating dollar in the September quarter: its cash is catching up with its build.
- Microsoft's purchases pass 85% of its operating cash in the September quarter: even the strongest cloud stops paying its way.
- The four's purchases top their operating cash for the September quarter or for 2026: the group stops paying, toward the debt side.
The Call expects the answer no.
