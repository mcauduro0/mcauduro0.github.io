THE QUESTION
Does the U.S. Energy Information Administration's Short-Term Energy Outlook of January 2027 put the average Brent crude oil spot price for the fourth quarter of 2026 above $105 a barrel?

HOW IT IS SETTLED
Rule: Read the fourth-quarter 2026 Brent spot average, in dollars per barrel, from the energy prices table of the January 2027 Short-Term Energy Outlook, where the quarter is history. The answer is yes if it is above $105.00; no if it is $105.00 or below. The outlook's forecasts for 2027, futures prices and prices reported in the press are read alongside and do not decide the test.
Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration, Short-Term Energy Outlook, January 2027 release (energy prices table, quarterly Brent spot average), scheduled for January 12, 2027 (https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/)
If the reading is unclear: The EIA schedules the January outlook for January 12, 2027. If it is not published by January 19, 2027, or its energy prices table does not give the fourth quarter of 2026, the test is inconclusive, and the Result says what was read and what was missing; the next reading is the February outlook, scheduled for February 9, 2027, which also carries the quarter as history. Figures are read as first published in the release read.
Test date: 2027-01-12 (January 12, 2027); the test closes at 00:00 UTC that day

THE PAGE: Part 15, "Oil Is the Headline. The Real Yield Is the Bill."
From February 27 to October 8, 2026, the 10-year Treasury yield rose 125 basis points, 115 of them real yield, while breakeven inflation rose 10, by DeepStack's reading of FRED. Retail diesel set a record in the week of September 21, 71% above the week of February 23, by the EIA's survey. The oil shock reaches the AI trade through the Fed and the real yield.
What the page says this test would show: The barrel: whether the EIA's January outlook puts Brent's fourth-quarter 2026 average above the $105 a barrel it forecast on October 6, 2026.
