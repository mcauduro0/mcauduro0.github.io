Nvidia

Nvidia runs through The Buildout as the supplier whose demand is real where chips meet paying customers and whose financing is the open question: talks to guarantee roughly $250 billion of lease and debt financing for an OpenAI campus, a year to pay for investment-grade customers, funding packages across its customer base, and a depreciation slide that put the B200 at 158% of its purchase price while its own schedule reaches zero around year five.

In The Buildout

Where Nvidia appears.

One line per story, from the story itself.

  1. Part 10The depreciation test

    An Nvidia slide put the B200 at 158% of its purchase price, the H100 at 58% and the A100 at 25%.

    The Bubble Is in the Denominator

  2. Part 08Cash, or a loop

    Nvidia now gives investment-grade customers a year to pay, up from 90 days, and some CoreWeave loan covenants tie to its OpenAI contract.

    AI Lenders Are Financing the End of Scarcity

  3. Part 06The megawatt premium

    Daniel Romero estimates that Nvidia will sell about 90,000 NVL72 racks during 2027.

    Power Doesn’t Depreciate. Its Premium Moves.

  4. Part 04Defense has a case

    That is the market’s split in one glance: Nvidia and Micron have been paid for scarcity; Alphabet, Meta and Oracle have been charged for financing.

    AI Spending Has Met Its First Real Bill

  5. Part 03Debt is the product

    His sharpest exhibit is that Nvidia was in talks to guarantee roughly $250 billion of lease and debt financing for an OpenAI data-center campus in Ohio, plus up to $350 billion for OpenAI’s chip purchases.

    The Machines Will Work. The Debt Might Not.

  6. Part 02The meter versus the map

    He asked what sits inside construction-in-progress, how the AI segment’s revenue is counted and where all the Nvidia GPUs said to be in short supply actually are.

    SpaceX Is Funding an AI Bet Before the Proof

  7. Part 01Lede

    Demand for Nvidia hardware is real where chips meet paying customers today, but the financing behind the buildout is less durable than a backlog makes it appear.

    Nvidia’s Demand Is Real. Its Financing Is Fragile.
