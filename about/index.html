About DeepStack

DeepStack doesn’t do neutral.

An independent publication about the money behind artificial intelligence. We follow the capital, the chips, the power and the people betting on all three, and then we tell you what we think.

Why DeepStack exists

The AI build-out is the largest capital-spending cycle of this generation, and most of what is written about it is either a press release or a prophecy.

DeepStack exists for readers who want neither. We take the figures that move markets, the filings, the guidance, the rate prints, the power contracts, and we put them in an argument. Every story presents the strongest case on each side. Then it takes one.

We are not here to be right about everything. We are here to be clear about what we believe, why we believe it, and what would change our mind. That is what makes a verdict useful.

Our standards

How a DeepStack story is built.

Every part of The Buildout follows the same anatomy, so you always know what you are reading: fact, argument, or opinion. The rules behind it are in our Editorial Notes.

  1. 01

    The numbers

    Dated, sourced figures up front, collected in a “By the numbers” panel and charted where it helps.

  2. 02

    The fight

    The best case for each side, attributed to the investors, economists and companies who make it.

  3. 03

    The DeepStack view

    A clearly labeled verdict. Opinion is never disguised as reporting.

  4. 04

    What to watch

    The dated events that will prove us right or wrong, gathered across the site in The Docket.

Partner with DeepStack

Editorial is not for sale. Attention is earned.

We work with a small number of brands whose products belong in a serious conversation about AI, capital and infrastructure: sponsored briefings, research collaborations and ambassadorships.

  • Labeled, always. Every sponsored piece is marked as sponsored, on the page and in the inbox.
  • No paid verdicts. Partners never review, approve or influence a DeepStack view.
  • Fit over reach. We decline partnerships that would not make sense to our readers.
